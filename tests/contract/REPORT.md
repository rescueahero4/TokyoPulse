## Notes on check 15 (degradation guarantee) design decision

`/forecast.json` (source: live Open-Meteo HTTP feed) and `/sandboxes.json` (source:
`ingest/state/sandboxes.json` on disk) do not read from Neo4j at all, so killing the
graph correctly leaves their `meta.degraded` unchanged — that is honest behaviour, not
a bug. The validator therefore requires `meta.degraded === true` only for the seven
endpoints whose tier-1 source actually is the graph (`/health`, `/events.json`,
`/lines.geojson`, `/stations.geojson`, `/brief`, `/layers.json`, `/impact/{lineId}`),
and requires zero 5xx responses across all nine endpoints regardless of source. Both
held on every run.

## Notes on check 16 (response time) — variance

`/impact/Toei-Mita` failed the 3s budget on every run observed (4.1s-9.2s across
roughly a dozen samples taken during this investigation, median about 5s) — the root
cause is the N+1 sequential Neo4j round trips in `api/graph.py` `fetch_impact()` (see
DEFECTS in the final report). The other endpoints times were noisy run-to-run
(anywhere from 0.4s to about 4.5s for a single Neo4j round trip), consistent with
variable network latency to the Neo4j Aura free-tier instance from this environment
rather than a code defect — they were fast (under 1.5s) on most runs including the one
pasted above, and are single-round-trip queries. `/impact/{lineId}` is structurally
different (4-6 serial round trips per request) and was never observed under 3s in any
sample.
